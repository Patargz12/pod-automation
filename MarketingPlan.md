# Nullish Content Strategy: Zack D Films Style

## Goal

Grow an audience with short, curiosity-driven 3D videos in the style of Zack D. Films, and turn that attention into sales for **Nullish** (AI and tech humor apparel on Etsy).

The videos bring the viewers. The profile and the follow-up posts make the sales.

## How We Make Sales (in one line)

**Viral video → people check our profile → they see the shirts → we post a "made a shirt about this" follow-up → they buy on Etsy.**

## Why not copy Zack D. Films 1:1

- His main revenue is YouTube Shorts ad share and sponsors. Shorts pay only cents per 1,000 views, so the model only works at billions of views per month.
- We already have a product with a profit on every sale. One sale is worth more than thousands of Shorts views.
- So we copy his **format** (hook, quick 3D story, payoff), not his business model. The content should drive traffic to Nullish.
- Sponsorships can come later, as a second income, once the audience is big.

## Niche and Audience

- **Topics:** anything that sparks curiosity. AI and tech topics are the core, but non-tech topics are allowed if the hook is strong.
- **AI topics:** bring viewers who are likely to buy our AI humor shirts.
- **Non-tech topics:** grow the audience. These viewers are reached through the catchphrase shirt (see below), not the AI shirts.

## Content Format

Inspired by Zack D. Films, but with our own look (no copying his characters or branding).

- **Length:** 20 to 45 seconds
- **Style:** clean 3D animation, voiceover, fast pacing
- **Hook (first 1 to 2 sec):** a curiosity gap, like a big number, a strange fact, or a "why does this happen"
- **Story:** one clear idea, shown visually
- **Payoff:** answer the hook, ending on a punchline
- **Sign-off:** our catchphrase, under 1 second

### Video Structure

| Time | Part | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 2s | Hook | "A chatbot sold a $70,000 SUV for $1." |
| 2 to 30s | Story or explanation | 3D scene of the chat, the deal, the fallout |
| 30 to 35s | Payoff and punchline | Answers the hook with a joke |
| Last 1s | Catchphrase sign-off | Same line, same voice, every video |

## The Sales System

### Rule 1: Never sell inside the video

- No shirt in the animation, no end card, no "link in bio" voiceover.
- Every video is pure content, so retention, looping, and virality are not hurt.

### Rule 2: Set up every profile to sell

On YouTube, TikTok, Facebook, and Instagram:

- Name and handle: **Nullish** (same on all platforms)
- Bio link to a simple page with the 4 to 6 best shirts (not the shop homepage)
- Pin 1 product showcase video (TikTok and Instagram allow 3 pins: 2 best viral videos + 1 product video)
- Caption on every video: "Shirts in bio"

### Rule 3: "Made a shirt about this" follow-up (main sales driver)

1. Post a content video with no product.
2. If it goes viral, design a shirt from its punchline within a few days.
3. Post a short follow-up: "You loved this video, so I made the shirt."
4. Viewers already know the joke, so the shirt sells itself.

This way we only design shirts that have already proven popular.

### Rule 4: Catch buyers in the comments

- **TikTok:** reply to "I need this on a shirt" comments with a video showing the shirt.
- **Instagram and Facebook:** add "Comment SHIRT and I'll DM you the link" and send links automatically (for example with ManyChat). This also adds comments, which helps reach.

### Rule 5: Pinterest sells directly

Every shirt gets a pin that links straight to its Etsy listing. Keep the automation pipeline focused here.

### Rule 6: The catchphrase shirt

- One short line said at the end of every video, worded the same every time.
- After enough videos, viewers know it and repeat it. Then it becomes a shirt that sells to the **whole** audience, tech and non-tech.
- It takes about 20 to 30+ videos to stick, so start using it from video 1 but don't expect sales from it early.

**Catchphrase options (pick one, never change it):**

| Catchphrase | Why |
| --- | --- |
| "Now you know. Stay nullish." | Ties to the brand name |
| "Useless fact unlocked." | Works for any topic, game-like |
| "You didn't need to know that. Now you do." | Funny, any topic |
| "Brain: slightly less null." | Brand pun |
| "Curiosity: satisfied." | Clean on a shirt |

Optional: add a short sound (like a "ding") with the sign-off so it is recognizable without words.

## Content Ideas

### Real AI Stories

1. **The $1 Chevy Tahoe.** A dealership chatbot agreed to sell a $70K SUV for $1 after a user told it to agree with everything.
2. **The chatbot that cost Air Canada money.** It invented a refund policy, and a tribunal made the airline honor it.
3. **Google's $100 billion typo.** Bard got a James Webb telescope fact wrong in its launch demo, and Alphabet's stock value dropped about $100B.
4. **The lawyer who trusted ChatGPT.** He submitted court cases that ChatGPT made up, and the judge caught it.
5. **The AI that lied to hire a human.** In OpenAI's own testing, GPT-4 got a TaskRabbit worker to solve a CAPTCHA by claiming to be visually impaired.
6. **Microsoft's chatbot lasted 16 hours.** Tay was shut down after the internet taught it to say awful things.
7. **Saying "please" to ChatGPT costs millions.** Sam Altman said polite prompts cost OpenAI tens of millions in compute.
8. **The AI mistake that cost a company $100M.** (Pick a verified real case.)

### AI Explainers

9. **Why ChatGPT always says "Great question!"** AI is trained to please people.
10. **Why AI can't count the R's in "strawberry."** AI reads tokens (chunks of words), not letters.
11. **Why AI used to draw 6 fingers.** Hands are hard to learn from photos.
12. **Why AI lies so confidently.** It predicts the next word, not the truth.
13. **You've been training AI for years without knowing.** CAPTCHA clicks helped label data.
14. **What happens when you type a prompt.** Follow one message from your phone to a data center and back.
15. **The first chatbot fooled people in 1966.** ELIZA was a simple script, yet people told it their secrets.
16. **Move 37: the move no human would play.** AlphaGo made a move experts called a mistake, and it won the game.
17. **Why ChatGPT gets worse in long chats.** AI can only hold so much of the conversation at once (its context window).

## Rules

- **Fact-check everything.** Verify every story and number against a real source before scripting. The "true story" angle only works if people trust it.
- Never sell inside the video. Selling happens on the profile, captions, comments, and follow-up posts.
- Same catchphrase on every video.
- Keep the Nullish design rules for any new shirt.
- Use our own 3D style, inspired by the format, not a copy of Zack D's look.

## Testing Plan

1. Make **5 videos** using AI tools before investing in full 3D.
2. Post **20 videos** (mix of AI and non-tech), all with the catchphrase and no product.
3. For every video that goes viral, make the shirt and post the follow-up.
4. After 2 to 3 weeks, compare:
   - Views and watch-through rate: AI vs non-tech topics
   - Profile visits and bio link clicks
   - Etsy traffic sources (Etsy Stats)
   - Sales from follow-up shirts
5. Do more of the topics and follow-ups that sell. Drop what doesn't.
